Village gets its first text-only library

By Ada Morven, Dunmore Gazette (a fictional newspaper written for textwire's tests)

Residents of Dunmore, a hill village with no mobile data signal, can now borrow the news by text message. The scheme, run from the old post office, answers any question sent to a single number with a short, readable page – and it costs nothing to ask.

“People here have phones, they just don’t have data,” said Morag Keir, who started the project after the last bus route was cut.[1] She said the idea came from reading about the history of SMS and the 160-character limit that shaped it.[2]

How it works

A text is sent to the library’s number. A computer in the post office fetches the answer, trims it to the words that matter and sends it back in a handful of messages. Longer pages arrive in parts, and readers can ask for the next part whenever they like.

  • Search the web by texting a few words
  • Open any page by texting its address
  • Follow a link by texting its number

What it costs

Each reply costs the library about six pence a message. The parish council has agreed to fund the first year, with a cap of two hundred messages a day.

  2. A news story usually takes twelve.
